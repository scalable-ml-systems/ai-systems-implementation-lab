## Findings

1. Host-to-device bandwidth increased from 1.72 GB/s at 1 MiB
   to approximately 2.0–2.1 GB/s at 16 MiB and above. This indicates
   that small transfers are more affected by fixed transfer/setup overhead,
   while larger transfers approach a sustained bandwidth plateau.

2. Device-to-host bandwidth ranged from 0.50 to 0.82 GB/s and was
   consistently lower than host-to-device bandwidth. At 1 GiB, D2H
   bandwidth was 0.82 GB/s versus 2.01 GB/s for H2D.

3. These measurements use pageable host memory allocated by std::malloc
   and synchronous cudaMemcpy calls. They characterize the end-to-end
   host↔GPU transfer path in this cloud VM, not GPU-local HBM bandwidth.

4. The asymmetry and modest sustained bandwidth may be influenced by
   pageable-memory staging, VM/virtualized transport behavior, host-memory
   performance, CPU scheduling, or shared cloud infrastructure. Further
   experiments with pinned host memory and repeated timed iterations are
   required before attributing a single root cause.
